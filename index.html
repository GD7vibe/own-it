The arithmetic

Weight goes up when more energy goes in than comes out. There is no third input.

Your body stores the surplus as fat. Roughly 7,700 kcal of surplus makes a kilo. Nobody gains weight on food they did not eat, and nobody keeps it on while eating less than they metabolise.

500 kcal a day too much  →  about 0.45 kg of fat a week  →  about 23 kg a year

The NHS guideline for an average adult is about 2,500 kcal a day for a man and 2,000 for a woman. A pint of lager, a chocolate bar and a skipped walk can cover the 500 without you noticing. That is how the weight arrives. It arrives slowly, through your own choices, one ordinary day at a time.

The three reasons

Find yours. Then you know where to start.

The words are blunt on purpose. Each one describes something you do, which means each one is something you can stop doing.

Greedy

Eating beyond what your body needs because it is there and it tastes good.

Where it shows up

  • Second helpings. Finishing the plate, and the kids' plates.
  • Calories you drink: beer, wine, sugary drinks, large milky coffees.
  • Grazing between meals out of habit rather than hunger.
  • Eating because you are bored, stressed or it is Friday.

What to do

  • Serve one portion. Put the rest away before you sit down.
  • Cut drinks first. They are the easiest calories to remove.
  • Eat at set times and stop snacking between them for two weeks.
  • Do not keep the stuff in the house.

Lazy

Sitting when you could stand. Driving when you could walk. Choosing the sofa.

Where it shows up

  • Under 5,000 steps on most days.
  • Driving for trips under a mile.
  • Evenings and weekends flat out in front of a screen.
  • No strength work, so muscle shrinks and you metabolise less at rest.

What to do

  • Walk every day. Build towards 8,000 to 10,000 steps.
  • Lift something heavy twice a week.
  • Take the stairs, park further away, stand for calls.
  • Put it in the diary. Movement that is not scheduled does not happen.

Unwise

Not knowing how it works, or knowing and ignoring it.

Where it shows up

  • Believing a walk cancels out a chocolate bar. It takes over two miles.
  • Trusting labels like "healthy", "low fat" or "natural".
  • Never weighing yourself and never counting anything.
  • Crash dieting, rebounding, and repeating.

What to do

  • Learn the calories in the twenty foods you eat most.
  • Weigh yourself weekly, same day, same time. Write it down.
  • Track everything you eat for two weeks. Most people undercount.
  • Aim for 0.5 to 1 kg a week. Slow wins.

Blame and ownership

Blaming is easy. It also does nothing.

Pointing at someone else feels good. It is free, it is quick and it brings instant relief. It does not move the scale by a single gram.

If it is somebody else's fault

  • The fix is somebody else's job.
  • You wait for them to change.
  • You feel better tonight and the same next year.

If it is yours

  • The fix is yours, so you can start tonight.
  • You have agency over what you eat, drink and do.
  • Your body is the most important thing you will ever own, and you hold the controls.

One exception to rule out

See your GP first

A small minority of people have a medical factor that makes this harder: an underactive thyroid, steroid tablets, some antidepressants, PCOS. Get it checked. One GP appointment and a blood test will rule most of them in or out.

Once you know, what you do next is yours. Carrying on and doing nothing about it is the unwise part.

Whatever started it, staying overweight comes down to greedy, lazy or unwise. A medical cause changes how hard the job is. It does not change who has to do it.
